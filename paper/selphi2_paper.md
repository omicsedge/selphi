# **Selphi 2: genotype-likelihood imputation for low-coverage and capture sequencing, unified with array phasing and imputation in one binary**

Authors: Adriano De Marino^1^, Sandra Bohn^1^, Jon Lerga-Jaso^1^, Biljana Novković^1^, Andrew Terpolovsky^1^, Puya G. Yazdi^1,\*^

^1^ Research and Development, Omicsedge, Miami, FL, USA

\* Corresponding author: pyazdi@omicsedge.com

# **Abstract**

Imputation from one or two sequencing reads per site differs from array imputation: most sites carry a genotype likelihood rather than a genotype, and calling genotypes first discards both the uncertainty and the sites left uncalled. Here we present Selphi 2, a Rust tool whose genotype-likelihood-aware Li-Stephens engine imputes directly from likelihoods or aligned reads with a native pileup, and integrates phasing, array imputation, panel preparation and multi-format output in one whole-genome binary. On six Genome in a Bottle samples sequenced by hybridization capture over the ~614,000 Global Screening Array sites with a 1.6-2.7$\times$ off-target background, imputing from likelihoods instead of treating the libraries as an array raises non-reference concordance at untyped chromosome-22 sites from 68.27-75.38% to 97.32-98.17% (+25.09 percentage points; heterozygote recall +39.17 points; six of six samples). Against GLIMPSE2 on identical site lists and a leak-free 4,796-haplotype 1000 Genomes panel, Selphi 2 leads non-reference concordance in six of six samples with its native pileup (+0.19 points; +0.20 points, six of six, from bcftools likelihoods), replicates on chromosomes 20, 10 and 1 with the margin growing toward rare variants on the larger chromosomes, and imputes a sample from the BAM 3.3$\times$ faster (101 versus 334 s, 16 threads). On 30$\times$ GIAB genomes downsampled to ~1.8$\times$, its per-sample dosage $R^2$ exceeds GLIMPSE2's in six of six samples (0.9501 versus 0.9449), and across a 0.5-4$\times$ sweep it leads GLIMPSE2 and QUILT2 in overall $R^2$ at every depth. The same binary phases and imputes genotyping arrays, matching or exceeding Beagle 5.5 at equal phasing and exceeding Beagle's full pipeline with its own phasing on six consumer arrays against a 75,552-haplotype panel, with lower trio switch-error than SHAPEIT5, and completes the consumer-array genome in 61% of Beagle's wall time at under a sixth of its peak memory. It ships as one self-contained x86-64 and ARM64 binary writing five output formats from a single pass.

# **Introduction**

Genotype imputation infers ungenotyped variants from reference panels of sequenced haplotypes. It extends the variant coverage of array-genotyped and low-coverage sequenced cohorts at a fraction of the cost of deep whole-genome sequencing (WGS), and so underpins genome-wide association studies (GWAS), polygenic risk scores (PRS) and fine-mapping^1^. Reference panels have grown from thousands to hundreds of thousands of sequenced individuals, and rare and low-frequency variants have gained clinical weight. Two kinds of input now dominate: genotyping arrays, which give confident calls at a fixed set of typed sites, and low-coverage or targeted sequencing, which gives a thin, uneven layer of reads over every site.

At one or two reads per site, sequencing yields a genotype likelihood rather than a genotype: the reads fit more than one genotype, and their relative support is all the data contain. Calling genotypes first and imputing them as array calls discards that uncertainty, and discards outright the many sites where no call can be made. A likelihood-aware Li and Stephens model instead combines the read evidence at each site with the haplotype prior from the panel, so a single read shifts the posterior without fixing it. Hybridization-capture libraries, which pair deep coverage at the typed sites with a thin off-target background, raise the same problem at mixed depth. The array-equivalent route calls the typed sites, imputes them as a chip and discards the background; a likelihood-aware model uses both layers through one emission model.

Most imputation methods build on the Li and Stephens hidden Markov model (HMM)^2^, which reconstructs each target haplotype as a mosaic of segments copied from panel haplotypes, with switches between them standing in for historical recombination. To find the informative panel haplotypes among tens of thousands, current tools use the positional Burrows-Wheeler transform (PBWT)^3^. Three tools are in wide use. IMPUTE5^4^ selects haplotypes with the PBWT and scales sub-linearly with panel size. Beagle^5^ (here version 5.5) combines integrated phasing^6^ with haplotype clustering. Minimac4^7^ runs the Michigan and TOPMed imputation servers with low memory. All three impute common variants accurately; they differ in rare-variant handling, memory use and integration with phasing.

The array workflow is spread across several tools. A typical pipeline phases the target with Eagle2^8^, SHAPEIT5^9^ or an earlier biobank-scale phaser^10^, and imputes it with Beagle 5.5^5^, IMPUTE5^4^ or Minimac4^7^. The panel is converted between tool-specific formats (bref3 for Beagle, xcf for IMPUTE5, msav for Minimac4), the genome is split into per-chromosome files, outputs are indexed with bcftools, and results are re-encoded into BCF, Apache Parquet or PLINK2 PGEN^11^ by external writers. Each handoff adds intermediate files, shell scripts and per-chromosome bookkeeping. Beagle 5.5^5^ runs phasing and imputation in one Java process, but still one chromosome at a time and with VCF output only.

The original Selphi^12^ addressed a different limitation: the short matching range of the windowed and chunked PBWT scans used by other tools. Selphi 1 ran the PBWT along the whole chromosome and summed match evidence over the full scan, which raised rare-variant accuracy above Beagle 5.4, IMPUTE5 and Minimac4 on the 1000 Genomes Project^13^ and TOPMed^14^ panels. It was, however, written in Python with Numba-compiled inner loops, required pre-phased input, processed one chromosome at a time, and capped candidate haplotypes per variant at fixed values that do not scale with panel size.

Other recent methods address parts of the problem. SHAPEIT5^9^ reduced rare-variant switch error in large cohorts by phasing in two passes: a backbone of common variants, onto which rare alleles are then placed one at a time. GLIMPSE2^15^ made whole-genome low-coverage imputation tractable with a compact panel representation: dense bitmaps for frequent alleles and carrier lists for rare ones. QUILT2^16^ extended the PBWT to multi-symbol matching for diverse sequencing technologies. GLIMPSE2 and QUILT2 both target genomes sequenced at uniformly low depth. None of these tools combines phasing, imputation, panel preparation, indexing and multi-format output in one executable, and none imputes a whole genome in one process against one panel file.

Here we present Selphi 2, a Rust tool built around a genotype-likelihood-aware Li-Stephens engine (`--lcwgs`) that imputes from Phred-scaled likelihoods or directly from aligned reads with a native pileup. The same binary contains a reimplementation of the Selphi array pipeline. It keeps Selphi 1's long-range, match-accumulating PBWT selection but runs it in large overlapping windows (default 80 cM) rather than one whole-chromosome pass, which removes Selphi 1's main runtime bottleneck. Two phasing engines are built in: a diploid genotype-graph engine with MCMC sampling, the default for all inputs, and a haploid composite-HMM engine for chip arrays; pre-phased input is no longer needed. A whole genome is imputed with one command against one multi-chromosome panel file, and the next chromosome is prefetched while the current one is in the HMM, so no per-chromosome splitting or concatenation is needed. The number of panel haplotypes kept per target follows from panel size and haplotype diversity; on admixed cohorts imputed against biobank-scale panels this retains rare-allele carriers from minority ancestries that a fixed candidate-set size drops. A streaming sparse panel format keeps peak memory below 500 MB during panel construction regardless of panel size, and the Rust code reduces wall time and peak memory relative to Selphi 1 (Results). One interpolation pass writes any of five formats: VCF.gz, BCF 2.2, Apache Parquet, PLINK2 PGEN with dosage, and SelfDecode per-sample Parquet archives. Figure 1 summarises the pipeline.

We describe the algorithms (Methods) and then benchmark the likelihood engine on capture-plus-low-pass Genome in a Bottle libraries^17^, against the array-equivalent route and against GLIMPSE2^15^, and on uniformly downsampled Genome in a Bottle genomes against GLIMPSE2 and QUILT2^16^. We then compare the array pipeline's accuracy and resource use with Selphi 1^12^ and Beagle 5.5^5^ on the 1000 Genomes Project^13^ and TOPMed^14^ panels, and its phasing accuracy with SHAPEIT5^9^ on 1000 Genomes trios (Results).

# **Methods**

## **Selphi 2**

### **Architecture overview**

Selphi 2 is a single self-contained Rust binary. Compression libraries, format encoders (VCF, BCF, Parquet, PGEN) and index builders (TBI, CSI) are built in, and no external tool is called at run time. Parallelism uses the rayon library^18^ with fixed-seed iteration orders, so output is bit-identical across runs on the same hardware. The inner loops of the haploid and diploid HMMs are SIMD-vectorised for x86-64 and ARM, and a run-time CPU check selects the kernel.

The pipeline accepts unphased or phased VCF/BCF input, phases the target if needed, imputes against the panel and writes one or more output formats at once. Phased haplotypes pass to the imputation engine in memory, with no intermediate files. Memory use is estimated at startup from the panel dimensions and thread count, with a warning if it exceeds available RAM.

### **Phasing engines**

Selphi 2 has two phasing engines. The diploid engine is the default for all inputs: across our benchmarks it matches or exceeds the haploid engine on SNP accuracy, has equal or lower switch error, and phases the trio benchmark several times faster (Discussion). The haploid composite-HMM engine, designed for chip arrays, remains available as an option. Missing target genotypes, typically 1-2% of sites per sample on an array, are carried through both engines as missing and imputed from the surrounding haplotypes by a local copying consensus: the majority allele among the nearest neighbours in a positional-BWT sweep over the target and panel haplotypes. Setting them to the reference allele instead conditions the phasing scaffold on false homozygous-reference calls, which lowers downstream accuracy directly and by biasing the imputer's choice of panel haplotypes toward the reference allele at those sites (Results, Table 5; Discussion).

**Haploid engine.**

The haploid engine models each haplotype separately through a composite HMM with a greedy swap criterion, following Browning and Browning^19^. It runs 15 iterations: 3 burn-in iterations with fixed phase, then 12 phasing iterations whose likelihood-ratio threshold falls from 100,000 to 1.0.

Each iteration builds a coded-step PBWT^4^ on the combined panel and target haplotypes at the shared variants. Steps are fixed genetic-distance intervals on an adaptive scale: fine (scale 1.0, about 10 SNPs per step) in early iterations, when phase is uncertain, and coarse (scale 3.0, about 30 SNPs per step) later, once the scaffold is established. This resembles SHAPEIT5's common-then-rare strategy^9^ but within one iterative scheme rather than separate stages.

Within each iteration a composite HMM runs three parallel channels for alternative haplotype configurations. At each heterozygous site a greedy swap compares the forward-backward posteriors of the channels and is accepted if the likelihood ratio exceeds the current threshold, which falls across iterations in the manner of simulated annealing.

For rare variants, carrier injection keeps rare-allele carriers in the matching: when the best identity-by-state (IBS) match at a position lacks the target's rare allele, it is replaced by the carrier that best matches the target by IBS over 30 flanking variants on each side. This prevents rare alleles from being lost at the matching step.

EM parameter estimation^20^ is performed within each iteration to calibrate the mismatch probability and effective recombination rate per window, enabling the HMM transition probabilities to adapt to local recombination patterns.

**Diploid engine.**

The diploid engine models the two haplotypes jointly through a genotype graph, following SHAPEIT4^21^ and SHAPEIT5^9^. At each heterozygous site the graph encodes all local diplotype configurations as 64-bit bitmasks. A segment-based Li-Stephens HMM computes diplotype transitions across conditioning haplotypes chosen by positional PBWT; their number can be capped for speed (default: unlimited).

Phase is sampled by MCMC on the genotype graph over 15 iterations in the scheme 5b,1p,1b,1p,1b,1p,5m (5 burn-in, 3 interleaved prune and burn-in cycles, 5 main), followed by a final Viterbi solve. The forward pass is SIMD-vectorised. This engine phases common variants only (MAF ≥ 0.1%); rare variants are phased in a separate pass (Rare variant phasing, below).

By default the diploid engine forms a small **intra-run phase ensemble**, which integrates over phase uncertainty for one extra imputation pass and no extra phasing. Each main MCMC iteration draws a complete diplotype configuration, a sample from the phase posterior that the chain would otherwise discard in favour of the final Viterbi solve. The Viterbi solve and a thinned subset of these samples are kept as N phase members (default N = 2) from the one chain. Each member is carried through rare-variant phasing and imputation, and the per-haplotype Li-Stephens copying weights are averaged across members before interpolation. Because the dosage is linear in those weights (dosage = Σ w · panel allele, normalised by Σ w), the averaged weights give the members' mean dosage. The identity is exact up to the per-row sparsity threshold on the weights, whose effect we measured at $10^{-6}$ in per-sample $R^2$ on chromosome 22. The cost is one phasing plus N imputation passes, with the panel read and interpolation shared, rather than the N phasings of a conventional ensemble. On the consumer-array benchmark N = 2 adds ≈25-30 % wall time and raises the diploid engine's genome-wide SNP $R^2$ from 0.9588 to 0.9605 on top of the no-call fix (0.9609 in the current release; Results, Table 5). The ensemble is off (N = 1) under `--sample-batch-size` and `--phase-only`. The haploid engine does not use it, because its greedy swap converges to one solution rather than sampling a posterior.

During burn-in the effective population size ($N_e$) is re-estimated from the genotype graphs by inverting the Li-Stephens relation^2^ $\mathrm{rate} \approx 0.04 \times N_e \times d / n_\text{haps}$, where $d$ is the mean inter-marker distance in cM. The rate used is the number of diplotype-graph transitions per variant, an edge count rather than a fraction of sites, so the estimate overshoots and settles at its upper bound. That bound therefore sets the phasing $N_e$. For array targets it is $\min(77.5 \times n_\text{haps}, 10^6)$ with a floor of 1,000, so the per-cM switch rate is constant below 12,904 haplotypes and decays as $1/n_\text{haps}$ above; for targets at whole-genome density (at least 1,000 scaffold sites per Mb), including the trio benchmark, it is a flat $10^6$, because the scaled bound raised switch error on dense data. An update is applied only if it changes $N_e$ by more than 5%.

**Rare variant phasing (diploid).**

Rare variants, those outside the common-variant scaffold, are phased in a PBWT-based pass over the target haplotypes and, when supplied, the reference panel; panel phasing, with no external panel, uses the cohort alone. This common-then-rare split follows SHAPEIT5^9^, whose rare pass uses the cohort only.

The pass has three steps. First, an IBD2 scan finds sample pairs that share both haplotypes identical by descent across scaffold sites^22^, using a diploid PBWT on genotype sums (0/1/2) and requiring segments of at least 2.5 cM, 1 Mb and 100 scaffold sites. These pairs are excluded as each other's PBWT neighbours, since their haplotypes cannot resolve phase at heterozygous sites they share.

Second, a scaffold bitmatrix is built at scaffold sites from the target and, when present, the panel haplotypes, excluding sites with more than 10% missing data. A forward PBWT sweep visits all variants in physical order. At scaffold sites it updates the PBWT arrays (permutation A, divergence C, reverse lookup R); at rare heterozygous sites it phases by a two-pass vote. In the threshold pass the two PBWT neighbours of each target haplotype vote on the phase; if the net vote exceeds a threshold that falls from 2.5 to 1.0, the phase is assigned and the carrier joins later decisions. In the distance pass, sites still unphased are decided by votes weighted by the genetic distance from the PBWT divergence point to the current position.

Third, a backward PBWT sweep repeats the same procedure in reverse genomic order. The forward and backward results are merged by selecting the direction with higher confidence (absolute vote score) for each rare het.

Singletons (MAC = 1 among the targets) need no special rule. No PBWT neighbour carries the allele, so the threshold pass casts no vote and the distance pass places the allele on the haplotype with the shorter PBWT match to its neighbours. This is the direction of SHAPEIT5's coalescent argument for singletons^9^, that a recent allele sits on the more recently diverged haplotype, here derived from PBWT divergence rather than a coalescent HMM.

**Pedigree scaffolding.**

With a pedigree (PLINK PED format), Mendelian logic is applied to the genotypes before phasing. For each trio or duo the algorithm considers the nine combinations of parental genotype sums at each variant. A missing child genotype is filled when both parents are homozygous. When the child is heterozygous and at least one parent homozygous, the Mendelian phase is written to the input; by default the HMM then re-derives the phase at every heterozygous site, so this orientation initialises the sampler rather than constraining it. Fixing those sites as a scaffold, as SHAPEIT5 does^9^, is available but not the default. On 54 trios genotyped at 9,283 array sites it lowered imputation R², even though the fixed phase agreed with the children's sequenced haplotypes at 99.95% of sites: fixing four heterozygous sites in five lets genotype-graph segments grow to their length limit and removes the segment boundaries where the copying model can switch haplotype. Mendelian inconsistencies (for example a heterozygous child of two parents homozygous for the same allele) are counted but not corrected.

On chromosome X, haploid samples (males) are detected automatically by their heterozygosity rate: samples with fewer than 1% heterozygous calls across at least 100 non-missing sites are classified as haploid. Heterozygous calls in haploid samples are biologically impossible and indicate genotyping error; these are reset to missing to allow the HMM to impute the correct homozygous genotype.

**Panel phasing.**

Without an external panel, the same engines can phase a cohort against itself in one command. The cohort haplotypes serve as both target and conditioning set, with the diploid engine unless the haploid one is requested. Output is a phased VCF/BCF and, optionally, an SRP or BREF3^5^ file for direct use as a reference panel. If the cohort does not fit in RAM, the genome is chunked along the genetic map and the chunks are ligated at the boundary haplotype assignment.

### **PBWT matching for imputation**

Imputation uses a coded-step PBWT^4^ that works directly on the panel bitmatrix (1 bit per allele, packed in 64-bit words). At each step boundary, set from genetic-map positions about 0.05 cM apart, haplotypes are grouped by their allele sequence within the step, 64 haplotypes per word operation. Steps of at most 40 SNPs are coded exactly as a 64-bit shift register over their alleles; longer steps are coded by FNV-1a hashing. The resulting coded-step partitions are the basis for selecting candidate haplotypes.

For each target haplotype, candidates are chosen by how often they share the target's coded-step partition. The number kept per target (`max_candidates`, henceforth $m_c$) is set from panel size and diversity, as described next. Per-thread workspaces (permutation and divergence arrays) are allocated once and reused for every target on that thread. When the panel is large relative to the batch, the panel sort is shared by groups of targets and each target only scans the shared order for its neighbours. A target carrying the majority allele at a site visits only the minority-allele positions of its match block, located through a per-site index and block maxima of the divergence array. The matches found, and hence the output, are unchanged (byte-identical), and on MESA 5K $\times$ TOPMed chromosome 20 the run time fell from 10,767 to 9,047 s.

### **Candidate selection and HMM**

Candidates are filtered twice before the HMM. Those below the 10th percentile of occurrence across step partitions are removed, and those covering more than 95% of positions have their gaps filled, so that otherwise consistent matches are not truncated. Panel haplotypes identical at the shared positions are then merged (haplotype deduplication), which removes 10-50% of HMM states depending on the panel and region.

A reduced array of the candidates plus the target is built, and a forward-backward pass is run on it. Hidden-state vectors are 32-bit floats, for twice the cache density and SIMD throughput; per-site recombination probabilities and per-row normalisation sums are computed in 64-bit for numerical stability. The backward pass is streamed: each backward row is multiplied into the stored forward row as soon as it is computed, so only two backward rows are held at a time instead of a full site-by-state matrix. The per-site recombination probability follows the standard Li-Stephens form^2^:

$$P(\mathrm{switch}) = 1 - \exp\!\left(-d_k \times 0.04 \times N_e / n_\text{ref}\right)$$

where $d_k$ is the genetic distance between consecutive markers in cM, $N_e$ the effective population size and $n_\text{ref}$ the number of candidate haplotypes. Existing tools use one $N_e$ that does not scale with panel size; Beagle, for example, defaults to $N_e$ = 100,000 in both its phasing and imputation HMMs, optionally re-estimated once per run by EM^5^. The best $N_e$, however, grows with the panel. In a larger panel, PBWT-selected candidates match the target more closely and share shorter identity-by-descent segments with it, so the HMM must switch between candidates more often to follow the mosaic, which needs a proportionally larger $N_e$.

Selphi 2 therefore sets $N_e$ as a linear function of the total number of panel haplotypes:

$$N_e = \max(36.4 \times n_\text{ref,total},\ 100{,}000)$$

where $n_\text{ref,total}$ is the total number of panel haplotypes, not the number of candidates. The slope was fitted by maximising imputation $R^2$ on three panels spanning two orders of magnitude: 1000 Genomes Phase 3 (4,802 haplotypes; best $N_e$ ≈ 175,000), a UK Biobank^23^ and 1000 Genomes composite (75,552 haplotypes; ≈2,750,000) and TOPMed (171,054 haplotypes; ≈6,200,000). The ratio $N_e/n_\text{ref} \approx 36.4$ held across these panels despite their different composition (a global multi-ethnic panel, a UK Biobank composite and a multi-ethnic clinical cohort). A per-MAF sweep on the current implementation (Supplementary Table S13) confirms the optimum and shows its structure: on the 1000 Genomes panel overall $R^2$ peaks at the formula's $N_e \approx 175{,}000$, while rare variants prefer a lower value (≈50,000) and common variants a higher one (≈350,000). The formula balances a real trade-off between rare and common variants.

$N_e$ is further adjusted per site by MAF: it is $0.85 \times N_{e,\text{base}}$ for rare variants (MAF < 0.5%), $1.2 \times N_{e,\text{base}}$ for common variants (MAF > 2%), and ramps linearly in between. With the haploid phasing engine, the per-window $N_e$ estimated during phasing replaces this scheme in the imputation HMM unless disabled; with the default diploid engine the scheme applies as written. A fixed $N_e$ can be set to reproduce earlier results.

**Adaptive candidate-set sizing.** Selphi 1 kept conditioning haplotypes with fixed per-variant match caps that did not depend on panel size: a forward top-$K_1$ = 450, reduced to $K_2$ = 60 matches per variant in a backward re-scoring pass, then a top-$K_3$ = 50 filter. Selphi 2 sizes the per-target candidate set ($m_c$) from panel size and diversity instead. A fixed $m_c$ truncates panels at rates that differ by two orders of magnitude: $m_c = 2{,}500$ keeps 50% of a 5,000-haplotype panel but 1.5% of a 171,054-haplotype panel. In the second case the top candidates are dominated by the panel's majority ancestry, rare-variant carriers from minority sub-populations are excluded, and admixed cohorts lose $R^2$ at low frequencies.

Selphi 2 resolves $m_c$ automatically as

$$m_c = \mathrm{clamp}\!\left(N_\text{ref}\,(\gamma + \alpha\,\mathrm{CV}_\text{panel}),\ m_c^{\text{floor}},\ m_c^{\text{ceil}}\right)$$

where $N_\text{ref}$ is the total number of panel haplotypes, $\gamma$ a base fraction, $\alpha$ a diversity-coupled fraction, and $\mathrm{CV}_\text{panel} \in [0, 1]$ the coefficient of variation of compressed tile sizes in the SRP, computed once when the panel is loaded. $\mathrm{CV}_\text{panel}$ stands in for haplotype diversity: panels with several distinct mosaic structures (multiple ancestries, divergent sub-populations) compress less uniformly than homogeneous ones and give a higher CV.

**Choice of $\gamma$, $\alpha$, and the small-panel rule.** Subsetting the conditioning set saves time and memory only on biobank-scale panels. On a small panel it discards rare-allele carriers for no computational gain, and the diversity-coupled fraction can return only part of the panel (on a low-CV panel it falls to the floor). We therefore keep the **entire panel** as the conditioning set up to 16,000 haplotypes (≈8,000 samples), and apply $m_c = \mathrm{clamp}(N_\text{ref}(\gamma + \alpha\cdot\mathrm{CV}_\text{panel}),\ 2{,}500,\ 10^6)$, with $\gamma = 0.10$ and $\alpha = 0.80$ and a cap at $N_\text{ref}$, only above that size. The threshold lies above 1000-Genomes-scale panels, below biobank panels (HRC ≈ 65,000, TOPMed ≈ 171,000 haplotypes), and near the size at which accuracy saturates. It matters most for ancestries absent from the panel. Imputing HGDP individuals against a 1000 Genomes panel, the formula returned the 2,500 floor (39% of the 6,332-haplotype panel), dropped rare-allele carriers and inverted the rare-variant ranking against Beagle 5.5; keeping the full panel restored Selphi 2's lead at every frequency above the rarest bin (Results, Out-of-panel generalization). On the panels evaluated here the rule keeps all 4,802 haplotypes of 1000 Genomes Phase 3 and $m_c = 132{,}676$ of TOPMed ($\mathrm{CV}_\text{panel} = 0.845$, $N_\text{ref} = 171{,}054$; 78% of the panel). A fixed $m_c$ can be set to reproduce earlier results.

The HMM posterior weights are stored as sparse CSR matrices (one row per chip variant, one column per panel haplotype), with entries below $1/(H+1)$ set to zero.

### **Interpolation and output**

Between consecutive shared (chip) positions, dosages at panel-only positions are computed by linear interpolation of the HMM haplotype weights, as is standard^4,7^:

$$P(\mathrm{alt} \mid j) = \frac{\sum_r w_r(j)\, x_r(j)}{\sum_r w_r(j)}$$

where $w_r(j)$ is the interpolated weight of panel haplotype r at position j and $x_r(j)$ its allele there. Panel alleles at imputed positions are read from the tiled SRP format (below), with preloaded stripe batches overlapping I/O with the HMM.

Interpolation works on tiles of 1,024 variants $\times$ 4,096 haplotypes, sized to fit each core's L2 cache. Each tile's dosages are formatted to all active output formats as soon as they are computed and the tile is then freed, so interpolated dosages never accumulate over a chromosome.

The per-variant dosage R-squared (DR2) quality metric^24^ uses a numerically stable two-pass computation:

$$\mathrm{DR2} = \frac{\mathrm{var}(\mathrm{dosage})}{2\,p\,(1-p)}$$

where $p = \mathrm{mean}(\mathrm{dosage}) / 2$ is the estimated alternate-allele frequency and var(dosage) the mean squared deviation from the mean, avoiding the unstable single-pass $E[X^2] - E[X]^2$.

VCF formatting performs no floating-point-to-string conversion. Dosages are quantised to the emitted precision (three decimals for DS, two for AP), and the quantised values index byte-string lookup tables built once at start-up. A 2 $\times$ 2 $\times$ 2,001-entry table over (haplotype-1 call, haplotype-2 call, dosage index) writes a whole `GT:DS` field with one memory copy, and a 101 $\times$ 101-entry table does the same for `AP1:AP2`. Every sample at every variant takes this path, so formatting cost per sample is constant.

One interpolation pass writes up to five formats: VCF.gz (multi-threaded BGZF), native BCF 2.2^25^, Apache Parquet (zstd-compressed, variant-major columns), PLINK2 PGEN^11^ (mode 0x03, fixed-width records with 2-bit hard calls and 16-bit unphased dosage) and SelfDecode format (per-sample chunked Parquet in a ZIP archive). VCF/BCF output goes through a dedicated writer thread on a bounded channel, so interpolation and I/O proceed independently; the channel holds 16 blocks below 1,000 samples and 4 at or above, because one block at biobank scale is hundreds of megabytes of text. All formats use the same two-pass DR2 computation.

### **Low-coverage sequencing imputation engine**

The pipeline above is built for arrays, whose input is confident hard calls at chip sites. Low-coverage whole-genome sequencing (lcWGS) poses a different problem. At 0.5-4$\times$, and at the off-target sites of a capture library, most sites carry a soft genotype likelihood (GL) from one or two reads rather than a confident call, while deeply covered sites give near-certain likelihoods through the same model with no special handling. Selphi 2 has a dedicated engine for this, selected with `--lcwgs`. It reuses the panel format (SRP), the `HaplotypeBitmatrix`, the genetic map and the output writers, and replaces the hard-call HMM with a likelihood-aware Li-Stephens model. GLIMPSE2^15^ showed that this regime is best served by imputing from likelihoods rather than hard calls, and we compare against it in Results.

The engine reads per-sample Phred-scaled likelihoods (the `PL` field from `bcftools mpileup | call`) at the panel sites and converts them to per-haplotype likelihoods through a 256-entry lookup table. Alternatively it computes the likelihoods from aligned reads (BAM or CRAM, one file per sample) in the same process, with no external pileup. For each biallelic panel SNP, the overlapping reads are walked through their CIGAR alignments and each base enters the three genotype likelihoods under the revised MAQ error model of SAMtools/BCFtools^25^. That model caps base quality by mapping quality and by neighbouring-base quality, and down-weights repeated reads of the same base and strand to account for correlated errors. Reads are filtered by mapping and base quality (defaults MAPQ ≥ 20, base quality ≥ 20, at most 250 reads per site; `LCWGS_MIN_MAPQ`, `LCWGS_MIN_BQ`, `LCWGS_MAX_DEPTH`), and the result matches `bcftools mpileup` at the same thresholds. The two mates of one fragment sample the same molecule, so overlapping mates are collapsed to one observation per site (agreeing bases keep the higher quality, disagreeing bases are down-weighted). CRAM bases are reconstructed against the supplied reference FASTA, and with a region restriction the index (`.bai`/`.crai`) limits decoding to that span.

Panel sites other than SNPs, where the pileup collects no evidence, are excluded from the native target set and imputed from the haplotype scaffold, as in GLIMPSE2 and on the likelihood-file path restricted to SNPs. Carrying them as flat-likelihood sites instead changes the Table 1b result within noise (+0.184 against +0.190 pp non-reference concordance over GLIMPSE2). At low coverage, per-read indel likelihoods are miscalibrated and degrade nearby SNPs in the joint model. For higher-coverage input, an option builds the local REF and ALT haplotypes of each panel indel and scores every spanning read against both with a pair hidden Markov model (forward algorithm, affine gaps, base-quality emissions, marginalising over alignment), adding $P(\text{read}\,|\,\text{REF})$ and $P(\text{read}\,|\,\text{ALT})$ to the likelihoods as for a SNP. To check the native pileup against the external `bcftools mpileup` path, we downsampled a 52$\times$ genome to 1$\times$ with the full-depth calls as truth: the native pileup reached aggregate $r^2$ 0.964 per allele-frequency bin, against 0.960 from precomputed likelihoods and 0.967 for an equivalent GLIMPSE2 pileup.

Imputation alternates haplotype selection and a GL-weighted forward-backward in a Gibbs sampler. Each iteration selects a conditioning set of panel haplotypes by sparse PBWT from the currently sampled haplotypes. It then runs a Li-Stephens forward-backward in which each haplotype's emission at a site is conditioned on the partner haplotype's current allele, which splits a diploid sample into two independent copying problems, and samples a new allele per site from the posterior. The selected set is capped at 2,000 haplotypes per sample (`LCWGS_KPBWT`). With the default storage-site spacing of 0.002 cM the union of PBWT neighbours over a 16 cM chunk covers nearly the whole panel (5,708 to 6,332 of the 6,332 haplotypes of the coverage-sweep panel; 3,956 to 4,796 of the 4,796 of the capture-library panel). The cap, applied in haplotype-index order, therefore keeps an almost fixed low-index subset, exactly haplotypes 0 to 1,999 in several measured chunks, and the target-specific part of the conditioning set is the per-haplotype rare-carrier augmentation described below. A cap in PBWT match-rank order (`LCWGS_QUAL_TRUNC`; `g2` reproduces GLIMPSE2's order) raises the 0.5$\times$ ultra-rare bin of Table 2b from 0.8994 to 0.9059, above GLIMPSE2's 0.9023, on each of three seeds. It lowers the 5-10% bin at 0.5$\times$ and the ultra-rare and 1-2% bins at 1$\times$, where the default already leads, so the index-order cap stays the default. Posterior dosages from the post-burn-in iterations are averaged and written as VCF.gz with `GT:DS:GP`. The forward-backward uses a leave-one-out emission: the forward emission at each site is divided out of the posterior before the read likelihood and error model are re-applied, so each site's reads count once.

*Base alignment quality.* The native pileup applies base alignment quality (BAQ)^26^ as `bcftools mpileup` does by default. Extended BAQ is computed by a bit-exact port of htslib's `probaln_glocal` and `sam_prob_realn` (on these libraries all 28,596 tested reads receive base qualities identical to `samtools calmd -r -E`). It is applied to the reads that bcftools 1.22's partial-realignment heuristic selects: a column triggers realignment on indel or soft-clip evidence, and each read is judged once, at its first triggering column. On the capture libraries 7.6% of reads are realigned. Without BAQ the native pileup trailed the bcftools arm of the head-to-head (Table 1b: +0.125 against +0.197 pp over GLIMPSE2); with BAQ the two are level (+0.190 pp). GLIMPSE2's pileup applies no BAQ.

*Gibbs schedule, phasing and recombination model.* Each target sample is imputed independently. Three components run at every Gibbs iteration besides the capped selection above. An eight-founder phasing HMM re-commits phase (opt-out `LCWGS_NO_FOUNDER_PHASE=1` reverts to a heuristic diplotype-mosaic segment commitment). The imputation forward-backward uses a band-mode recombination rate (default; the phasing HMM keeps the conditioning-set-independent rate everywhere). At common sites the rate does not depend on the conditioning-set size, which gives a sticky copying model that carries a panel haplotype across sites with no reads. At rare sites the rate is raised to the per-haplotype panel value ($0.04\,N_e/n_\text{ref}$), so a copied haplotype can switch onto a true rare-allele carrier instead of staying on a flanking non-carrier. This is the change that brings the ultra-rare bin level with or above GLIMPSE2 (Results, Table 2b); `LCWGS_RECOMB_DENOM=max` restores the uniform rate. Finally, a rare-carrier augmentation (opt-out `LCWGS_NO_RARE_CARRIER`) appends, at each rare site, that site's panel carriers to the conditioning set of every target haplotype currently sampled with the minor allele, so a true carrier becomes copyable once the sampler has tentatively placed the allele. The augmentation is per haplotype, and under the cap the appended carriers rank behind the haplotype's selected states, leaving the common-site copying model intact. The forward matrix is stored only at $\sqrt{n}$ checkpoint columns and recomputed block by block in the backward pass. The number of Gibbs iterations and the cM window size trade accuracy for speed.

*Likelihood-file input.* Records without a `PL` field, such as the genotype-only indel records that `bcftools call -C alleles` emits when the alleles file has indels but `mpileup` ran with `-I`, are read as flat-likelihood sites. A `PL` VCF should therefore contain only records with likelihoods (`bcftools view -v snps`), as in every likelihood-file run reported here, or the BAM path should be used (Limitations).

To bound memory regardless of chromosome length and panel size, the chromosome is processed in overlapping cM chunks. Each chunk's panel bitmatrix is extracted from the SRP for that chunk's variants and discarded after use, so the full-chromosome bitmatrix never exists. The forward-backward loops over conditioning states use AVX-512, AVX2 or NEON selected at run time, with a scalar fallback; the AVX-512 path loads 16 bit-packed panel alleles directly as a mask register. The selection step accumulates IBS match lengths in a sparse per-target map rather than a dense target $\times$ panel matrix, so its memory does not grow with panel size.

### **Sparse Reference Panel format (SRP)**

The SRP format stores panel haplotypes as a 2D tiled sparse matrix. Each tile covers 1,024 variants $\times$ 4,096 haplotypes and is stored as a compressed sparse column (CSC) sub-matrix with u16 row indices and u32 column pointers, compressed with zstd at level 3. Tiles are laid out stripe by stripe (all bands of stripe 0, then stripe 1), so a batch of stripes is read with one pread() call.

SRP creation is streaming. Source data (BCF, VCF or BREF3^5^) is processed chunk by chunk; each chunk is decompressed once and scattered to tile columns in parallel, and finished stripes are written immediately, which keeps peak memory below 500 MB for any panel size. The parallel BCF reader uses the CSI index^25^ to split the file into balanced genomic regions across threads. The reverse conversion is also built in: an SRP or BREF3 panel can be decoded to phased VCF or BCF by a streaming three-stage pipeline (panel read, record formatting and BGZF compression on separate threads) with bounded memory. This removes the need for Beagle's `unbref3` and its intermediate files, and the decode is lossless (Results).

An SRP can hold one chromosome or many. A multi-chromosome SRP has a global header with a chromosome directory and shared sample IDs, followed by independent per-chromosome tile sections; it is detected automatically. It can be built from a multi-contig BCF, from a directory of per-chromosome BCF files, or by merging single-chromosome SRP files.

### **Multi-chromosome processing**

Selphi 2 imputes a whole genome with one command against one multi-chromosome SRP.

The target VCF is read once and split by chromosome in memory. Genetic maps are found in a directory by matching chromosome names against common patterns (chr1.map, chr_1.map, genetic_map_chr1_*, and so on). Chromosomes run in natural sort order, and the next chromosome's panel data is prefetched on a background thread during the current one's HMM, which hides I/O latency at the cost of one chromosome's panel data.

Output is one VCF/BCF file across all chromosomes, with the per-chromosome BGZF blocks concatenated natively so that block alignment for tabix/CSI indexing is preserved.

### **LD correction**

Genetic map distances in the HMM transitions are corrected for local linkage disequilibrium (LD)^27^. Empirical switch rates between consecutive variants in the panel are computed by XOR and popcount on the bitmatrix, normalised by expected heterozygosity, median-filtered, and used to adjust the map distances while preserving total genetic length. This prevents an inflated effective population size in regions of strong LD.

### **Reference panels and relatedness**

We used six reference panels. The **1000 Genomes Phase 3** panel (2,401 samples, 4,802 haplotypes) is used with its published phasing; the 801 evaluation targets are a disjoint held-out subset. The **no-trios** panel (2,239 samples, 4,478 haplotypes) is the 1000 Genomes cohort with all declared trio children and parents removed; it serves the phasing benchmark and the downsampled low-coverage benchmark (Table 2). The **out-of-panel** benchmark uses the 3,166 1000 Genomes samples of the gnomAD Human Genome Diversity Project and 1000 Genomes harmonized callset (6,332 haplotypes) as the panel and the 925 HGDP individuals as targets; the same panel serves the coverage sweep (Table 2b). The **75,552-haplotype** panel (37,776 samples) combines 34,582 UK Biobank genomes, ancestry-balanced by down-sampling the majority European set, with the full 1000 Genomes cohort (3,194 samples), phased with SHAPEIT5^9^; it is approximately 49% European, 22% South Asian, 19% African, 10% East Asian and 1% admixed-American. The **TOPMed Freeze 8** panel (171,054 haplotypes, 85,527 samples) excludes the MESA study samples, so the 5,000 MESA targets are held out from it. The **NA12878-trio-removed 1000 Genomes** panel (2,398 samples, 4,796 haplotypes) serves the capture-library benchmark (Tables 1-1d). It is the NYGC 30$\times$ GRCh38 call set of the 2,401 samples not among the 801 array targets, with NA12878, NA12891 and NA12892 removed and alleles longer than 255 bp dropped (1,070,399 chromosome-22 sites: 929,834 SNPs and 140,565 indels, 54,406 of them monomorphic in the retained samples). None of HG002-HG007 is in any panel. A seventh capture library (NA12878) was sequenced on the same design but is not reported, because a possible relative (NA12872) remains in the panel and the version of its truth set is unrecorded. For chromosomes 20, 10 and 1 the panel was further restricted for both tools to sites polymorphic in the retained samples with alleles ≤ 250 bp (chromosome 20: 1,564,145 sites, 1,357,434 SNPs and 206,711 indels); on chromosome 22 only GLIMPSE2 received the polymorphic-site restriction (1,015,993 sites) that its BAM input requires (Limitations).

As is standard for imputation panels, related individuals were kept. Unlike an association cohort, where cryptic relatedness inflates test statistics, a reference panel gains from related haplotypes: they raise the local minor-allele count available to the copying model and so improve rare-variant accuracy, which is why 1000 Genomes, HRC and TOPMed are distributed without relatedness pruning. Only exact duplicates were removed from the 75,552-haplotype panel. The relatedness that can bias a benchmark is between a panel and its *evaluation targets*, and we control it on the target side. The GIAB and HGDP targets are absent from their panels; the 1000 Genomes targets share haplotypes with the panel by descent, which we state with those results (Results), and for this reason the leak-free GIAB and HGDP results are the decisive accuracy evidence.

### **Accuracy evaluation of genotype imputation**

Array-imputation benchmarks were scored with one evaluator (`selphi --evaluate`, or the same code inline through `--truth`) for every tool in a comparison, on the same sites. Per-variant $R^2$ is the squared Pearson correlation, across targets, between imputed dosage and truth genotype at a site, averaged within bins of minor-allele frequency computed from the truth genotypes of the targets. The OVERALL row is the corresponding pooled value over all scored sites. Per-sample $R^2$ is the correlation across sites within one target, averaged over targets. Typed array sites are excluded. A site where the truth varies but a tool's dosage is constant scores $R^2$ = 0. Treating it as undefined would drop, separately for each tool, exactly the sites that tool failed to impute, so each tool would be scored on a different and flattering site set. Sites monomorphic in the reference panel were excluded from every comparison (`--exclude-panel-monomorphic`): their alternate allele exists only in the held-out targets, so no tool can impute them, and when they are counted they fill the 0.1-0.2% bin of the 1000 Genomes benchmarks with zeros. This removes 54,076 of 1,070,401 sites on 1000 Genomes chromosome 22, 6,815,267 of 17.9 million on TOPMed chromosome 20, and no site on the 75,552-haplotype panel. Paired comparisons use per-sample differences, with 95% intervals from 20,000 paired bootstrap resamples and two-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank tests. The low-coverage benchmarks and the consumer arrays are scored as described in their own sections.

### **Consumer-array benchmark: data preparation and evaluation**

For the consumer-array benchmark (Results; Table 5) we used six individuals with both a SelfDecode consumer genotyping array (Illumina Global Screening Array, GRCh38) and an independent high-coverage Illumina genome processed with the DRAGEN germline pipeline (GRCh38; ≈30-60$\times$). All six were confirmed absent from the panel before any analysis.

*Truth construction.* DRAGEN hard-filtered calls were normalised and decomposed with `bcftools norm -m-any` against GRCh38 and restricted to high-confidence genotypes (`FILTER=PASS`, `GQ ≥ 20`, `DP ≥ 10`). This removed about 13% of raw calls on average (9% to 14% per sample; for example 4.98M to 4.34M in one sample). `FILTER=PASS` alone kept many low-confidence calls, with `GQ` as low as 5. SNPs and indels were both kept and evaluated separately. The high-confidence set defines the truth genotype at every site it contains.

*Array input.* Array genotypes were mapped to the panel's variant coordinates: REF, ALT and position were taken from the panel, and the genotype from the array alleles, complementing strands where needed. Homozygous-reference calls, which a naïve TSV-to-VCF conversion leaves with an undefined ALT and drops, were kept explicitly, giving ≈620,000 autosomal SNPs per sample. The six samples were merged and imputed as one cohort.

*Imputation.* The cohort was imputed against the 75,552-haplotype panel, with every tool given the same per-chromosome genetic maps. In the "Beagle-phased" rows, Selphi 2 and Selphi 1.5.3 received Beagle's phase-only output as input, which isolates imputation from phasing; the "haploid" and "diploid" rows use Selphi 2's own phasing. Beagle 5.5 (build 03 Oct 2025) ran as a full phase-and-impute pipeline against the same panel in `bref3` form.

*Evaluation.* For each sample, variant class (SNP, indel) and chromosome, $R^2$ is the squared Pearson correlation between imputed dosage and truth genotype. The truth genotype is the high-confidence call where one exists. Sites in the raw genome but removed by the confidence filter are excluded rather than counted as homozygous reference; sites absent from the genome are homozygous reference; and the array sites are excluded so that only imputed variants are scored. Reported values are means over the six samples across chromosomes 1-22. Tools are compared by the mean per-sample difference in $R^2$, a 95% interval from 20,000 paired bootstrap resamples of the six differences, and a two-sided exact Wilcoxon signed-rank test, whose smallest attainable p at n = 6 is 0.031.

### **Capture-plus-low-pass GIAB benchmark: data, processing and evaluation**

*Libraries.* Six GIAB samples (HG002-HG007; HG002 = NA24385, HG003 = NA24149, HG004 = NA24143, HG005 = NA24631, HG006 = NA24694, HG007 = NA24695) were sequenced by Gene by Gene, Ltd. (Houston, TX, USA) on an MGI DNBSEQ instrument (2 $\times$ 150 bp; adapters pre-trimmed; 27-46 M read pairs per sample) from a hybridization-capture library with baits on the 613,711 autosomal biallelic Global Screening Array (GSA) SNP sites. Reads were aligned with bwa-mem 0.7.19-r1273 (`-K 100000000 -Y`) to GRCh38_full_analysis_set_plus_decoy_hla and processed with samtools 1.22 `fixmate`, `sort` and `markdup` (duplicates marked, not removed; 4.8% in HG002); coverage was profiled with mosdepth 0.3.10. Of the reads, 71-73% fall within ±150 bp of an array site. On-target depth is 28.1-52.1$\times$, the off-target background 1.6-2.7$\times$, and the autosome-wide mean including on-target reads 2.68-4.71$\times$ (chromosome 22: 2.38-4.17$\times$); 43-45% of bases fall in GSA ±100 bp windows covering 4.08% of the autosomes. On chromosome 22, 506,862-572,999 of the 929,834 panel SNP sites (54.5-61.6%) carry no read at MAPQ ≥ 20 and base quality ≥ 20. The typed sites are the 613,711 autosomal biallelic ACGT GSA SNPs (9,144 on chromosome 22).

*Array-equivalent route.* Genotypes were called only at the GSA sites with bcftools 1.22 (`mpileup -a AD,DP -q 20 -Q 20 -d 500` restricted to those sites, piped to `call -m -A -C alleles`). No-calls were dropped (8,672-8,721 called chromosome-22 sites per sample), and the genotype VCF was imputed with `selphi` at its defaults, which route it to the array engine.

*Likelihood routes.* For the likelihood-file arm, bcftools 1.22 `mpileup -I -E -a DP -q 20 -Q 20` at the panel SNP sites was piped to `call -Aim -C alleles` with a SNP-only alleles file (929,834 rows on chromosome 22), and the `PL` VCF was imputed with `selphi --lcwgs --input`. For the native arm the BAM was imputed directly with `selphi --lcwgs --bam` (MAPQ ≥ 20, base quality ≥ 20, BAQ as above). Both arms used the unfiltered 1,070,399-site chromosome-22 panel.

*GLIMPSE2.* GLIMPSE2 v2.0.0 (commit 2cee597, 6 May 2026) ran from the BAM (`GLIMPSE2_phase --bam-file`) at its defaults (5 burn-in and 15 main iterations, $N_e$ = 100,000, Kinit = 1,000, Kpbwt = 2,000, MAPQ ≥ 10, base quality ≥ 10, maximum depth 40) over 16 chromosome-22 chunks run in sequence with `--threads 16`, followed by `GLIMPSE2_ligate`. Its `--bam-file` path aborts on panel-monomorphic sites (Limitations), so it received the panel restricted to its 1,015,993 polymorphic sites; on chromosomes 20, 10 and 1 both tools used the polymorphic-only panel.

*Truth.* We used each sample's GIAB v4.2.1 benchmark VCF and high-confidence BED: the per-sample BEDs on chromosome 22, and on chromosomes 20, 10 and 1 the `*_noinconsistent.bed` files for HG002-HG004 and the `*_benchmark.bed` files for HG005-HG007.

*Scoring.* Candidate sites are the SNPs emitted by GLIMPSE2 (head-to-head, Tables 1b-1d) or by Selphi 2 (Table 1). Typed GSA sites are removed first (9,018 on chromosome 22 in the head-to-head lists), and a site is kept if it lies in the high-confidence BED; a kept site with no truth record is homozygous reference. Sites with a multi-allelic truth or call, or a non-reference truth allele that does not match the panel alleles, are skipped (59-75 per sample), and no-calls are removed from the denominators. Overall concordance is the fraction of sites whose call matches the truth exactly. Non-reference concordance is the fraction of truth heterozygous or homozygous-alternate genotypes recovered exactly; it measures carrier recall and does not penalise false non-reference calls, which appear in heterozygote precision and overall concordance. Heterozygote recall is correct heterozygote calls over truth heterozygotes, and heterozygote precision the same over called heterozygotes. Between 94.4% and 94.8% of sites are homozygous reference in truth, so overall concordance plays the role of the per-sample $R^2$ of the downsampled benchmark, and per-MAF strata accompany every aggregate. MAF is $\min(\mathrm{AF},\,1-\mathrm{AF})$ from `bcftools +fill-tags` on the 2,398-sample panel, in three strata: below 0.5%, 0.5-5% and 5% or more.

*Statistics.* As for the consumer arrays: mean paired difference in percentage points (pp), 95% interval from 20,000 paired bootstrap resamples of the six differences (numpy `default_rng`, seed 1) and a two-sided exact Wilcoxon signed-rank test (minimum p = 0.031 at n = 6). Where only a paired t is given (Table 1d), it is the mean difference over its standard error across the six samples.

*Timing.* Wall time and peak memory were measured with `/usr/bin/time -v` on an otherwise idle 16-core host, one job at a time, from BAM to imputed VCF for both tools; GLIMPSE2's time is the sum of its chunk, phase and ligate steps.

*Determinism.* Re-running the six chromosome-22 samples with a later build reproduces every concordance statistic to four decimals.

Comparators ran at their defaults unless noted, with the same per-chromosome genetic maps and the same reference panel as Selphi 2 in each benchmark, and imputed variants were scored on the same site set for all tools compared. # **Results**

We used two low-coverage datasets and kept them separate. The first is real capture-plus-low-pass libraries of six GIAB samples, scored by genotype concordance against a 2,398-sample leak-free panel (Tables 1-1d). The second is 30$\times$ GIAB genomes uniformly downsampled to 0.5-4$\times$, scored by dosage $R^2$ (Tables 2, 2b). The two use different panels and site lists, and their statistics are not interchangeable. We then evaluated genotyping-array imputation against Beagle 5.5 and the original Selphi 1.5.3 on three panels spanning two orders of magnitude: 1000 Genomes Phase 3^13^ (4,802 haplotypes), TOPMed Freeze 8^14^ (171,054 haplotypes), and a 75,552-haplotype (37,776-sample) panel combining 34,582 UK Biobank whole genomes with the full 1000 Genomes cohort (3,194 samples). Imputation $R^2$ was computed against held-out whole-genome truth and stratified by panel minor allele frequency (MAF). All benchmarks ran on one machine with 16 threads.

## **Genotype-likelihood imputation of capture-plus-low-pass libraries: a regime change over array-equivalent processing**

Six GIAB samples (HG002-HG007, the Ashkenazi and Han Chinese trios; HG002 = NA24385, HG003 = NA24149, HG004 = NA24143, HG005 = NA24631, HG006 = NA24694, HG007 = NA24695) were sequenced by Gene by Gene on an MGI DNBSEQ instrument (2 $\times$ 150 bp) from a hybridization-capture library over the 613,711 autosomal biallelic Global Screening Array (GSA) SNP sites (Methods). The libraries combine deep typed sites with a thin background. Of the reads, 71-73% fall within ±150 bp of an array site. On-target depth is 28.1-52.1$\times$, the off-target background 1.6-2.7$\times$, and the autosome-wide mean 2.68-4.71$\times$ (chromosome 22: 2.38-4.17$\times$). The GSA ±100 bp windows cover 4.08% of the autosomes but hold 43-45% of the bases. On chromosome 22, 506,862-572,999 of the 929,834 panel SNP sites (54.5-61.6%) carry no read at MAPQ ≥ 20 and base quality ≥ 20. These are real libraries with strongly uneven depth, not downsampled genomes, and we use them to test likelihood imputation at mixed depth, the regime of array-plus-low-pass products. The panel is the 2,398-sample (4,796-haplotype) NYGC 1000 Genomes 30$\times$ GRCh38 call set with the NA12878 trio removed (Methods); none of HG002-HG007 is a 1000 Genomes sample. The 9,144 typed GSA sites on chromosome 22 are excluded from every untyped-site statistic.

Each library was processed by two routes through the same binary and panel. The array-equivalent route calls genotypes only at the array sites and imputes them through the standard phase-and-impute path, as for a genotyping array. After dropping no-calls it keeps 8,672-8,721 chromosome-22 sites per sample; at the 8,042-8,320 evaluable typed sites the call rate is 95.6-96.2% and concordance with the GIAB truth 99.57-99.72% (mean 99.66%). The likelihood route (`--lcwgs`) imputes from bcftools genotype likelihoods at all 929,834 panel SNP sites. Both are scored on Selphi 2's own site list (700,109-723,251 untyped sites per sample) against the GIAB v4.2.1 high-confidence truth.

Imputing from likelihoods transforms the result (Table 1; Figure 2a,b). Non-reference concordance at untyped sites rises from 72.78% to 97.88% (per sample 68.27-75.38% to 97.32-98.17%; mean +25.09 pp, 95% CI +23.23 to +27.18; six of six samples, p = 0.031). Heterozygote recall rises from 57.95% to 97.12% (+39.17 pp, CI +36.61 to +41.86), heterozygote precision from 92.75% to 97.40% (+4.64 pp, CI +4.00 to +5.30), and overall concordance from 98.50% to 99.83% (+1.33 pp). The gain holds in every panel-MAF stratum. Non-reference concordance rises from 74.19% to 98.58% at MAF ≥ 5%, from 65.67% to 95.42% at 0.5-5% and from 54.65% to 86.76% below 0.5%; heterozygote recall rises from 60.34% to 98.27%, 44.00% to 93.03% and 30.85% to 79.87%. The difference is the information the array route discards, the off-target background and the deep flanking coverage, and not tuning of two imputers. ## **Head-to-head with GLIMPSE2 on the capture libraries**

We then compared the likelihood engine with GLIMPSE2^15^ (v2.0.0) on the same six libraries. Both tools imputed from the same BAM files against the same leak-free 2,398-sample panel and were scored on identical per-sample site lists: GLIMPSE2's emitted chromosome-22 SNPs inside the high-confidence regions, with 9,018 typed sites excluded (662,250-684,142 sites per sample; truth heterozygous/homozygous-alternate genotypes HG002 24,458/12,797, HG003 24,988/12,775, HG004 22,453/12,221, HG005 21,526/14,422, HG006 19,159/16,258, HG007 22,437/14,606). GLIMPSE2 read the BAM directly (`--bam-file`, 16 chunks, ligated) against the panel restricted to its 1,015,993 polymorphic sites (Methods). Selphi 2 ran in two arms: its native pileup (`--bam`, MAPQ ≥ 20, base quality ≥ 20, base alignment quality applied), which is the like-for-like comparison and the result we report for the engine, and bcftools-mpileup likelihoods, which is what a deployed pipeline would produce. Agreement between the two arms shows that the native pileup reproduces the established likelihood computation.

In the like-for-like arm Selphi 2 leads GLIMPSE2 in non-reference concordance in six of six samples (97.74% to 97.93%; mean +0.19 pp, 95% CI +0.10 to +0.31, p = 0.031; per sample +0.07 to +0.46 pp) and in heterozygote recall in six of six (96.94% to 97.22%; +0.28 pp, CI +0.16 to +0.45, p = 0.031) (Table 1b; Figure 2c). Heterozygote precision (97.27% to 97.40%; +0.13 pp, CI +0.00 to +0.26) and overall concordance (99.81% to 99.82%; +0.01 pp, CI +0.01 to +0.02) are higher in five of six samples. The bcftools arm gives the same means to within 0.01 pp: non-reference concordance +0.20 pp (97.94%; CI +0.11 to +0.32; six of six), heterozygote recall +0.28 pp (97.22%; six of six) and heterozygote precision +0.13 pp (CI -0.06 to +0.34; four of six). Base alignment quality (BAQ) brings the native pileup to this parity. Without BAQ, as before it was implemented, the like-for-like arm gave +0.13 pp (five of six; CI +0.02 to +0.25; p = 0.094) and +0.01 pp in the rare stratum; with BAQ, which `bcftools mpileup` applies by default and GLIMPSE2's pileup does not (Methods), it gives +0.19 pp.

By panel MAF, the like-for-like advantage is unanimous at common sites but not consistent per sample below 5% (Table 1c; Figure 2d). At MAF ≥ 5% (79,005-81,576 sites per sample) non-reference concordance is 98.39% for GLIMPSE2 and 98.58% for both Selphi 2 arms (+0.19 pp, CI +0.10 to +0.30, six of six). At 0.5-5% (108,535-112,020 sites) it is 95.15% against 95.33% native and 95.42% bcftools (native +0.18 pp, CI -0.08 to +0.45, four of six). Below 0.5% (474,710-490,546 sites, 71.7% of those evaluated) it is 87.71% against 87.97% and 87.99% (native +0.26 pp, CI -0.03 to +0.60, three of six). Heterozygote recall follows the same pattern. On chromosome 22, then, Selphi 2 leads consistently at common sites; below 5% the mean favours it in both arms, but the intervals include zero and the per-sample ordering varies. The larger chromosomes resolve this stratum (next section). Per-sample $R^2$ on downsampled genomes (Table 2) gives the same picture at chromosome-22 scale.

The engine is also faster from the BAM. Timed in one session on an idle 16-core host, with GLIMPSE2 timed from chunking through its 16 phased chunks to ligation, one chromosome-22 sample takes 101 s from BAM to imputed output for Selphi 2 (peak memory 4.9 GB) against 334 s for GLIMPSE2 (2.1 GB), 3.3$\times$ faster (two repeats each, within 0.7 s). Because each sample is imputed independently, several can share one run. Six libraries take 170 s (28 s per sample; 13.4 GB) against 386 s for GLIMPSE2 (64 s per sample; 8.2 GB), 2.3$\times$; twelve samples (the six libraries plus six relabelled copies, for timing only) take 307 s (26 s per sample; 14.8 GB) against 451 s (38 s per sample; 17.2 GB), 1.5$\times$. At GLIMPSE2's iteration count (20 Gibbs iterations, 5 burn-in and 15 main) the six-sample run takes 75 s (13 s per sample) with unchanged accuracy (+0.20 pp over GLIMPSE2, against +0.19 pp at the default). GLIMPSE2's own accuracy is the same in single- and six-sample runs (difference below 0.01 pp). Memory per sample is higher here than in the downsampled benchmark (4.9 against 4.2 GB; Table 2) because this panel has 1,070,399 sites, 140,565 of them indels, while that one is SNP-only with 4,478 haplotypes.

## **Replication across chromosomes**

To test whether the chromosome-22 result generalises, we repeated the comparison on chromosomes 20, 10 and 1 with the same samples, truth and scoring. Both tools used one shared panel per chromosome restricted to polymorphic sites with alleles ≤ 250 bp (2,398 samples; chromosome 20: 1,564,145 sites; Methods), GLIMPSE2 at its defaults and Selphi 2 in both arms (Table 1d; Supplementary Figure S1). The native pileup leads GLIMPSE2 in non-reference concordance in six of six samples on every chromosome: +0.18 pp on chromosome 20 (97.72% to 97.91%; 1,139,422-1,168,950 sites per sample; paired t = 2.79), +0.21 pp on chromosome 10 (98.21% to 98.42%; 2,555,483-2,586,705 sites; t = 3.90) and +0.26 pp on chromosome 1 (98.20% to 98.46%; 2,216,536-2,263,597 sites; t = 3.20), against +0.19 pp on chromosome 22 (t = 3.15). The bcftools arm is also six of six on every chromosome (+0.21, +0.23 and +0.26 pp). Heterozygote recall rises with it (native +0.20, +0.32 and +0.38 pp; six of six on chromosomes 10 and 1, five of six on chromosome 20). Pooled over the four chromosomes and site-weighted within each sample (6,573,691-6,703,394 untyped sites per sample), the native arm leads by +0.22 pp in non-reference concordance (t = 3.49; six of six) and +0.31 pp in heterozygote recall (t = 2.61; six of six), and the bcftools arm by +0.23 pp (t = 3.67; six of six).

The rare-variant result sharpens with chromosome size. On chromosome 20 the stratum below 0.5% gives +0.48 pp (four of six). On chromosomes 10 and 1 it is the largest of the three strata and unanimous: +0.41 and +0.66 pp (six of six), against +0.20 and +0.23 pp at MAF ≥ 5% and +0.24 and +0.37 pp at 0.5-5%. The inconsistency seen below 0.5% on chromosome 22 therefore does not generalise; on the larger chromosomes the advantage grows toward rare variants. Heterozygote precision is within noise on chromosomes 22, 20 and 1 (native +0.13, +0.17 and +0.13 pp) and lower than GLIMPSE2's on chromosome 10 in both arms (native -0.48 pp; three of six), the one metric on one chromosome where Selphi 2 does not lead. BAQ contributes on every chromosome tested: without it the native pileup gave +0.11 pp (five of six) on chromosome 20 and +0.16 pp (six of six) on chromosome 10, against +0.18 and +0.21 pp with it.

## **Controlled-coverage complement: downsampled GIAB genomes and a 0.5-4$\times$ sweep**

The capture libraries are uneven in depth, so we complemented them with uniformly downsampled sequencing of the same six GIAB samples. This benchmark differs from Tables 1-1d in panel (the 4,478-haplotype no-trios panel), site list and metric (per-sample dosage $R^2$), and its values are not comparable with the concordances above. The public NovaSeq PCR-free 30$\times$ alignments (brain-genomics-public, GRCh38) were downsampled to ~1.8$\times$ over chromosome 22. Genotype likelihoods were computed from the reads at the panel sites and imputed against the 4,478-haplotype (2,239-sample) no-trios 1000 Genomes panel, the leak-free panel also used for trio phasing below. Per-sample dosage $R^2$ against the GIAB v4.2.1 high-confidence truth was computed over the high-confidence variant sites imputed by both Selphi 2 and GLIMPSE2^15^. Selphi 2 ran with its defaults: band-mode recombination, founder-HMM phasing and rare-carrier augmentation at every Gibbs iteration (Methods).

Selphi 2 exceeds GLIMPSE2 in per-sample $R^2$ on all six samples: 0.9457 versus 0.9389 (HG002), 0.9522 versus 0.9447 (HG003), 0.9489 versus 0.9440 (HG004), 0.9465 versus 0.9416 (HG005), 0.9546 versus 0.9496 (HG006) and 0.9526 versus 0.9504 (HG007); mean 0.9501 versus 0.9449 ($\Delta$ = +0.0052; Table 2). The 95% interval from 20,000 paired bootstrap resamples is [+0.0039, +0.0065], and with n = 6 the two-sided exact Wilcoxon signed-rank test gives p = 0.031, its minimum. Below 0.5% MAF GLIMPSE2 is marginally ahead at this coverage (pooled over the six samples, 0.8788 against 0.8810), matching the chromosome-22 rare stratum of the capture libraries (Table 1c). Selphi 2 imputes one chromosome-22 sample in 117 s on 16 cores at 4.2 GB peak memory, against 332 s for GLIMPSE2 from chunking to ligation (2.8$\times$), timed in the same session; GLIMPSE2's one-time reference splitting, like building Selphi 2's SRP, is excluded from both.

## **Imputation accuracy on a biobank-scale admixed cohort**

A fixed candidate-set size keeps a shrinking fraction of the panel as the panel grows: $m_c$ = 2,500 keeps 1.5% of the 171,054-haplotype TOPMed panel. We expected such a cap to exclude haplotypes that carry rare alleles in minority sub-populations, and tested this on the MESA cohort^28^ (5,000 multi-ethnic samples genotyped on a chip array) imputed against the TOPMed Freeze 8 panel (chromosome 20, 17.9 million variants). The cohort is admixed (African, Hispanic, Asian and European sub-populations) and fully held out from TOPMed.

The panel-adaptive formula (Methods, Candidate selection and HMM) gives $m_c$ = 132,676 for TOPMed (tile-compression diversity CV = 0.845, scaled fraction 0.776). Every tool was scored by the same evaluator on the same 11,085,368 sites, the variants polymorphic in the panel (6,815,267 of the 17.9 million carry no alternate allele among the panel's haplotypes and cannot be imputed by any tool). At this size Selphi 2's default diploid engine reaches overall $R^2$ 0.6209 on MESA against 0.5916 for Beagle 5.5 ($\Delta$ = +0.0293). Per-sample mean $R^2$ is 0.9016 against 0.8842 ($\Delta$ = +0.0174), and Selphi 2 leads in all four self-reported ancestry groups (+0.015 to +0.032, largest for the East-Asian group; Figure 3b). At their defaults on the same set, Selphi 1.5.3, IMPUTE5 and Minimac4 reach 0.4957, 0.4947 and 0.4802: IMPUTE5's fixed $L$ = 4 conditioning states, Minimac4's block-based state reduction and Selphi 1.5.3's fixed per-variant match caps keep too few rare-allele carriers from a 171,054-haplotype panel, the truncation that the candidate-size ablation isolates. Selphi 2 leads all four tools in every MAF bin (Supplementary Table S11).

To isolate the sizing rule, we ran Selphi 2 on the same cohort with $m_c$ fixed at 2,500. On a 500-sample MESA subset (chromosome 20, TOPMed), $m_c$ = 2,500 gave overall $R^2$ 0.5680 and the automatic $m_c$ = 132,676 gave 0.6215, a gain of +0.0535 from candidate-set size alone (Table 4).

## **Out-of-panel generalization to ancestries absent from the reference panel**

The MESA experiment fixes the panel and varies the candidate-set size. A harder test is whether the advantage holds for ancestries absent from the panel. We built an out-of-panel benchmark from the gnomAD Human Genome Diversity Project and 1000 Genomes harmonized callset (22 autosomes, GRCh38, phased)^29^. The 3,166 1000 Genomes samples (6,332 haplotypes; African, admixed-American, East-Asian, European and Central/South-Asian ancestries) formed the panel, and the 925 HGDP individuals were held-out targets. Targets were masked to a common-SNP array (common variants thinned to approximately one per 5 kb), imputed back to full density and scored against their sequence genotypes. Two HGDP groups have no representation in the panel, Oceanian (n = 30) and Middle-Eastern (n = 157); others belong to represented continents but unrepresented populations (for example Central/South-Asian, n = 183).

Across the 22 autosomes Selphi 2 (default diploid engine) exceeds Beagle 5.5 in per-sample $R^2$ in all seven continental regions, including the two absent from the panel (Oceanian +0.0107, Middle-Eastern +0.0067; African +0.0132, East-Asian +0.0080; Table 4c). Per-variant $R^2$, scored on the sites polymorphic in the panel, is also higher overall (0.5356 against 0.5169) and in every MAF bin, the rarest included (0.05-0.1%: 0.3610 against 0.3495). Both tools' outputs were read in full on every chromosome (records written equal records scored). The advantage therefore extends to ancestries not in the panel. This benchmark also prompted the small-panel conditioning rule (Methods). When the sizing formula subset this 6,332-haplotype panel to 2,500 haplotypes, the rare-allele carriers of the out-of-panel targets were dropped and the per-variant ranking inverted; conditioning on the full panel gives the result reported here.

## **Independent validation against leak-free GIAB truth**

The 1000 Genomes benchmarks share haplotypes between panel and targets by descent, which can inflate accuracy. For independent validation we imputed the GIAB samples HG002-HG007, none of which are in the 1000 Genomes Project, against the 75,552-haplotype panel, which contains the full 1000 Genomes cohort. Truth came from the GIAB v4.2.1 high-confidence regions restricted to common variants (MAF 5-50%). Both tools imputed the same phased chip input against the same panel content (Beagle 5.5 received the bref3 file the SRP was built from), and both were scored on the same sites: 69,806 on chromosome 21 and 409,391 on chromosome 1.

On chromosome 21 Selphi 2 reaches $R^2$ 0.9700 (concordance 0.9803) against 0.9633 (0.9783) for Beagle 5.5 (build 03 Oct 2025); on chromosome 1, 0.9771 (0.9864) against 0.9757 (0.9860). This truth covers only common variants, where modern imputers are expected to converge; the leak-free test of rare-variant accuracy is the HGDP benchmark above, where Selphi 2 leads Beagle 5.5 at every frequency.

Resource use differs more. Timed in one session, one job at a time, Selphi 2 imputes chromosome 21 in 6.6-7.2 s at 3.2 GB peak memory against 11.4-12.3 s for Beagle 5.5 (15.4 s on the first, cold-cache run) at 7.4-9.5 GB, and chromosome 1 in 38.2-41.3 s at 6.5-6.9 GB against 43.7-45.4 s (95.2 s cold) at 21-44 GB (four runs each; Supplementary Table S3). Selphi 1.5.3, measured in May 2026 in a separate session, took 112.8 s at 7.1 GB on chromosome 21 and 1,923 s on chromosome 1, about 48$\times$ Selphi 2's time.

## **Imputation accuracy on consumer arrays against high-coverage WGS truth**

To test the deployment case directly, a consumer array imputed against the panel and checked against the same person's genome, we assembled six held-out individuals with both a SelfDecode genotyping array (Illumina Global Screening Array, ≈620,000 autosomal SNPs after restriction to panel sites) and an independent high-coverage Illumina DRAGEN genome (≈30-60$\times$). The arrays were imputed against the 75,552-haplotype (37,776-sample) panel, and the dosages were compared across chromosomes 1-22 with each individual's genome restricted to high-confidence genotypes (DRAGEN PASS, GQ ≥ 20, DP ≥ 10). None of the six is in the panel.

With the same Beagle-derived phasing for both tools, Selphi 2's imputation exceeds Beagle 5.5's across the 22 autosomes and six samples: mean SNP $R^2$ 0.9601 against 0.9584 and indel $R^2$ 0.7911 against 0.7840 (Table 5). At equal phasing Selphi 1.5.3 and Selphi 2 are level on SNPs (0.9599 against 0.9601) and Selphi 1.5.3 is higher on indels (0.7932 against 0.7911). With its own phasing, Selphi 2 exceeds Beagle's full pipeline with either engine. The default diploid engine has the highest value of any configuration (SNP 0.9609, indel 0.7939), above Beagle 5.5 (0.9584 / 0.7840), the haploid engine (0.9589 / 0.7910) and the Beagle-phased configuration (0.9601 / 0.7911). Two changes produced this. First, earlier versions folded array no-calls (≈1.7% of typed sites on this array) to homozygous reference before phasing, which biased the phasing scaffold and the choice of panel haplotypes toward the reference allele at exactly the sites being imputed. Imputing the no-calls instead, by a copying consensus over the nearest PBWT neighbours in both engines, closed the common-SNP gap with Beagle (Methods; Discussion). Second, the diploid engine averages its imputation over a small phase ensemble taken from post-burn-in samples that its MCMC chain already computes. Because interpolation is linear in the per-haplotype copying weights, averaging these dosages integrates over phase uncertainty at the cost of one extra imputation pass and no extra phasing (Methods). Together the two changes raise the diploid engine's SNP $R^2$ from 0.9553 in an earlier single-phase version to 0.9605; the current release measures 0.9609. Selphi 2 therefore no longer needs an external phaser to reach Beagle-level accuracy on real arrays. With six individuals we report paired statistics (95% intervals from 20,000 paired bootstrap resamples; two-sided exact Wilcoxon signed-rank test, whose minimum attainable p at n = 6 is 0.031). All rows were scored by the same evaluator on the same 162,741,172 SNP site-sample pairs. With its own diploid phasing Selphi 2 exceeds Beagle 5.5 on SNPs ($\Delta R^2$ +0.0025, [+0.0014, +0.0037]) and indels (+0.0099, [+0.0081, +0.0120]; both p = 0.031, six of six samples), and exceeds the haploid engine (SNP +0.0020, [+0.0018, +0.0023]; indel +0.0029, [+0.0027, +0.0032]; both p = 0.031). ## **Phasing accuracy on the 1000 Genomes trio dataset**

We evaluated phasing on 54 1000 Genomes trio children whose parents are independently sequenced, so the parent-of-origin phase of each heterozygous site follows from Mendelian inheritance. The children's genomes for chromosomes 1 and 22 were phased against the 2,239-sample 1000 Genomes panel with all trio members removed (the no-trios panel). SHAPEIT5 v5.1.1 ran its full pipeline (`phase_common` at MAF ≥ 0.001, then `phase_rare` on the common-variant scaffold) and Beagle 5.5 its default phasing. SER measured with a pedigree is not independent of the phasing, because the truth phase comes from the same parental genotypes. A method that fixes the child's phase to the Mendelian configuration at every informative site reaches a SER near zero by construction, as SHAPEIT5's `--pedigree` mode does here (0.000%). Selphi 2 uses the pedigree as a soft scaffold, and Beagle 5.5 sees the parents only as cohort members; both still phase by HMM where the trio is uninformative (both parents heterozygous), so their residual SER remains meaningful. The parent-free comparison (Table 6) is therefore the unconfounded measure, and there the tools are close. On chromosome 22 Selphi 2's diploid engine is indistinguishable from Beagle 5.5 (paired Δ = -0.019 pp, 95% CI [-0.049, +0.009], 20,000 paired bootstrap resamples; lower in 30 of 54 trios) and lower than SHAPEIT5, with the interval excluding zero (Δ = -0.078 pp, [-0.107, -0.050], 40 of 54 trios). ## **Output-format equivalence and reproducibility**

Selphi 2 writes every output format from one interpolation pass (Methods), so the format does not change the imputed values. On the 1000 Genomes chromosome-22 benchmark (801 samples, 1,070,401 variants) we compared $2.7\times10^{7}$ dosages (all 801 samples over one megabase). They are identical across VCF, BCF, Apache Parquet and PLINK2 PGEN up to each format's precision: BCF, Parquet and SelfDecode carry the full 32-bit dosage (Parquet against PGEN maximum $|\Delta|$ = $6.1\times10^{-5}$, exactly PGEN's 16-bit step), and VCF rounds to three decimals (maximum $|\Delta| \le 1.0\times10^{-3}$ against the binary formats). Hard calls follow each format's documented convention (Methods). Output is bit-reproducible: two runs of the full chromosome on the same hardware gave byte-identical VCF.gz (identical MD5), as the fixed-seed iteration orders imply (Methods). The panel codec is lossless: decoding the SRP panel to BCF, re-encoding it and decoding again reproduced the genotypes byte for byte (identical genotype-matrix MD5 over 1,070,401 variants $\times$ 2,239 samples), so the built-in converters replace the external bref3, xcf and msav round trips without loss. The capture-library results are also deterministic: re-running the six chromosome-22 samples with a later build from the same likelihood files reproduced every concordance statistic to four decimals.

# **Discussion**

Selphi 2 is a Rust tool built around a genotype-likelihood-aware Li-Stephens engine for sequencing at low or uneven depth, combined with array phasing and imputation in one whole-genome binary. On real capture-plus-low-pass libraries of six GIAB samples, imputing from likelihoods instead of array-equivalent hard calls raises non-reference concordance at untyped chromosome-22 sites by 25.09 pp and heterozygote recall by 39.17 pp in every sample. Against GLIMPSE2 on identical site lists the engine leads non-reference concordance in six of six samples with its native pileup (+0.19 pp) and from bcftools likelihoods (+0.20 pp). The lead replicates on chromosomes 20, 10 and 1 (six of six on each; pooled +0.22 pp) and grows toward rare variants on the larger chromosomes, and the engine imputes a sample from the BAM 3.3$\times$ faster. On genomes downsampled to ~1.8$\times$ it leads GLIMPSE2 in per-sample $R^2$ in six of six samples (0.9501 against 0.9449), and across a 0.5-4$\times$ sweep it has the highest overall $R^2$ of the three tools at every depth.

The same binary reimplements the Selphi array pipeline^12^ in Rust. It integrates phasing and imputation, imputes a whole genome in one command against one multi-chromosome panel, and replaces Selphi 1's fixed per-variant match caps with a per-target candidate-set size that adapts to the panel. On the 1000 Genomes Phase 3 panel its full pipeline exceeds Beagle 5.5's in overall $R^2$ by 0.0198 on chromosome 22 and 0.0197 on chromosome 1, in every frequency bin, though it is slower there (Table 7). On the TOPMed panel it exceeds Beagle 5.5 by 0.0293 on the admixed MESA cohort and leads in all four ancestry groups (Figure 3b), an advantage that comes from the larger candidate set kept per target. The adaptive sizing recovers +0.0535 overall $R^2$ over a fixed candidate set of 2,500, more at rarer frequencies and most in the most admixed groups (Tables 4 and 4b). On leak-free GIAB truth against a panel containing the full 1000 Genomes cohort, Selphi 2 leads Beagle 5.5 on both chromosomes tested (+0.0067 and +0.0014 on common variants) at less than half its peak memory, and runs 17-48$\times$ faster than Selphi 1.5.3.

Selphi 2 differs in scope from the four main existing tools. Beagle 5.5^5,6^ integrates phasing and imputation in one Java process and is the most widely deployed end-to-end tool, but it processes one chromosome at a time, writes only VCF, needs its panel in bref3 format, and uses an effective population size that does not adapt to panel size. On the 1000 Genomes benchmarks it trails Selphi 2 by 0.020 in overall $R^2$, and on the admixed biobank cohort by 0.029. IMPUTE5^4^ scales sub-linearly through PBWT selection but needs phased input from an external phaser and runs per chromosome. Minimac4^7^, the engine behind the Michigan and TOPMed servers, has one of the smallest memory footprints but also needs phased input and runs per chromosome. Given the same phased target and panel on 1000 Genomes chromosome 22, both trail Selphi 2, whether it imputes from that phasing (0.4761) or runs its full pipeline (0.4864), against 0.4613 for Minimac4 and 0.4587 for IMPUTE5 (Table 3), the ordering Selphi 1 reported against the same tools. SHAPEIT5^9^ phases only; its two-stage common-then-rare strategy inspired the rare-variant pass of our diploid engine. On the 54-trio benchmark, with identical testable sites for every tool, the diploid engine has lower switch error than the full SHAPEIT5 pipeline on chromosome 22 (2.528% against 2.607%) and chromosome 1 (1.879% against 1.929%) in 0.56$\times$ and 0.57$\times$ its wall time (343 s against 607 s on chromosome 1). Beagle 5.5's phasing is level with it (chromosome 22 2.548% against 2.528%; chromosome 1 1.865% against 1.879%); neither leads on both chromosomes, but Selphi 2 uses 3.6-9.6$\times$ less memory and 0.41$\times$ the wall time.

Handling both kinds of input in one tool has precedent: Beagle 4.1^30^ accepted genotype likelihoods alongside called genotypes, a mode dropped in Beagle 5. Today no array imputer reads likelihoods, and GLIMPSE2 and QUILT2 have no path for called genotypes. What is new is one binary that routes each input to a dedicated engine and matches or exceeds the specialist tool in both regimes. Phased haplotypes also pass in memory to the Li-Stephens forward-backward, with no intermediate VCF. From Selphi 1^12^ the design keeps the long-range PBWT scan, now run in large overlapping windows (80 cM by default) instead of one chromosome-wide pass. An ablation on chromosome 22 (Supplementary Table S10) quantifies it: an IMPUTE5-like 8 cM window costs 0.020 in overall $R^2$, and the panel-adaptive effective population size ($N_e$ = 36.4 $\times$ $n_\text{ref}$) adds 0.009 over a fixed $N_e$ = 20,000. Chromosome 22 is shorter than 80 cM, so there the default window covers the whole chromosome, as Selphi 1 did. The main additions are the integrated phasing engines, the adaptive candidate-set sizing, the single-pass multi-chromosome workflow, and panel input in BCF, VCF or BREF3 with multi-format output.

GLIMPSE2^15^ and QUILT2^16^ target low-coverage sequencing, and there Selphi 2 is directly comparable. On real capture-plus-low-pass libraries it leads GLIMPSE2 in non-reference concordance in six of six samples on chromosome 22 with either pileup and on each of chromosomes 20, 10 and 1; on downsampled ~1.8$\times$ genomes it leads in per-sample $R^2$ in six of six; and it is 3.3$\times$ faster per sample from the BAM. Below 0.5% MAF on chromosome 22 the per-sample ordering in the capture libraries is not consistent and GLIMPSE2 is marginally ahead on the downsampled genomes (Tables 1c and 2), whereas on chromosomes 10 and 1 the rare stratum carries Selphi 2's largest and unanimous lead (Table 1d); in the coverage sweep the two are level in this stratum at 1$\times$ and Selphi 2 leads from 2$\times$ upward. GLIMPSE2 read the panel restricted to polymorphic sites (Limitations).

The phasing experiments separate the phaser's contribution from the imputer's. In a full phaser $\times$ imputer matrix on a 1000 Genomes array target (801 samples), the three phasings (Selphi 2, Beagle 5.5, SHAPEIT5) were practically interchangeable as imputation input: all pairwise overall-$R^2$ differences were $\le 0.003$ for a fixed imputer, a fifth of the imputer effect. The choice of imputer moved overall $R^2$ by +0.013 to +0.014 in favour of Selphi 2 whatever the phasing (Supplementary Table S7). This fits the near-equal switch error on common variants (Table 6): array sites are mostly common, where modern phasers agree, so phasing does not limit array imputation and Selphi 2's advantage belongs to its imputer. Re-phasing an already well-phased panel with Selphi 2 raised overall $R^2$ slightly for both imputers (+0.0019 for Selphi 2, +0.0034 for Beagle 5.5, no bin lower), so keeping a panel's published phasing, as we do throughout, costs little (Supplementary Table S8). Family information is the exception (Table 6c): when phasing adds information, here the parental genotypes, downstream imputation improves against an independent truth (+0.018 to +0.024 $R^2$). Phasing helps imputation through the information it adds, not through the choice among comparable phasers.

The low-coverage experiments make the matching point about the input. The 25.09 pp gap between the array-equivalent route and the likelihood engine on the same libraries is information, the off-target background and deep flanking coverage that a hard-called array VCF cannot carry. The two routes share the binary, the panel and the truth and differ only in what the target file can express; what an imputer can recover is bounded first by what its input keeps. The same experiments set a reporting rule we apply throughout. Aggregates dominated by homozygous-reference sites (94.4-94.8% of the evaluated sites here, and the monomorphic sites inside per-sample $R^2$) move by hundredths of a point when rare-variant behaviour moves by whole points. We therefore report every aggregate with its strata below 0.5%, 0.5-5% and 5% or more, and take the like-for-like arm, not the better of two pipelines, as the engine's result.

The work has several limitations. First, in the rarest 1000 Genomes bin (0.05-0.1%) of the genome-wide imputation-only comparison Minimac4 narrowly leads Selphi 2 (0.3491 against 0.3479; Supplementary Table S9), though Selphi 2 leads Beagle 5.5 and IMPUTE5 there and leads every tool in every other bin. The chromosome-wide PBWT statistic behind candidate selection favours long matches and may underweight single-carrier haplotypes whose informative segment is short. As deployed, with its own phasing, Selphi 2 leads in this bin as well (Table 3). Second, the diploid engine, the default, exceeds the haploid engine on SNP $R^2$ (+0.0020 genome-wide on the consumer arrays), has equal or lower switch error (the two differ by 0.003 points on chromosome 1), and phases the trio benchmark 4.7-5.1$\times$ faster. A single-phase diploid run trailed the haploid engine slightly on indels, because rare variants are phased in a separate pass, but the default intra-run phase ensemble (Methods) reverses that (indel $R^2$ 0.7939 against 0.7910 on the consumer arrays). We therefore use the diploid engine for all inputs and expose the haploid one through `--phasing-engine haploid`; no current rule predicts the better engine from cohort admixture alone. Third, the adaptive sizing rule (Methods, Candidate selection and HMM) has two constants ($\gamma$, $\alpha$) tuned on three panels (1000 Genomes, a UK Biobank subset, TOPMed); we have not validated them on a panel materially different from these. Fourth, keeping 78% of a 171,054-haplotype panel as candidates is costly on biobank-scale runs (1.34$\times$ Beagle's wall time on MESA 5K $\times$ TOPMed chromosome 20); where rare-variant accuracy is not the priority, a smaller fixed $m_c$ runs faster. Fifth, earlier versions of Selphi 2's own phasing trailed Beagle by ≈0.003 $R^2$ on common SNPs of the consumer arrays. We first read this as a general difference in phasing, but it was a single defect: array no-calls (≈1.7% of typed sites) were folded to homozygous reference before phasing instead of imputed, corrupting the phasing scaffold and the choice of panel haplotypes at exactly those sites. Imputing the no-calls during phasing, by a copying consensus over the nearest PBWT neighbours in both engines (Methods), closed nearly all of the gap, and the diploid engine's intra-run ensemble carried it past every other pipeline (Table 5). With its own phasing the diploid engine now exceeds Beagle on SNPs and indels and the Beagle-phased configuration on both (0.9609 / 0.7939 against 0.9601 / 0.7911; the SNP margin, +0.0008 in five of six samples, is small), so deployment does not depend on an external phaser. The diploid engine gained most: SNP 0.9553 to 0.9588 from the no-call fix, then 0.9605 with the ensemble (0.9609 in the current release). The consumer-array set has only six individuals, so per-sample variance is appreciable and the smallest differences (below 0.001) are not resolved. Finally, the MESA $\times$ TOPMed benchmark covers chromosome 20 only and the per-MAF panel breakdowns chromosomes 1, 21 and 22, although the 1000 Genomes and HGDP aggregates (Figure 3a; Table 4c) are genome-wide; the sizing rule on the X chromosome under sex-aware phasing remains to be characterised.

The low-coverage benchmarks have their own limitations. First, they rest on six samples from two trios, on chromosome 22 with replication on chromosomes 20, 10 and 1, and all six libraries come from one capture design, instrument and provider. Second, genotype concordance (capture libraries) and dosage $R^2$ (downsampled genomes) are complementary: they use different panels (4,796 against 4,478 and 6,332 haplotypes), site lists and denominators, and we do not combine them. Third, the capture libraries are capture-plus-background rather than uniform low-pass sequencing: 54.5-61.6% of the chromosome-22 panel SNP sites carry no read, and the array-equivalent route's absolute values are specific to the 4,796-haplotype panel. Fourth, with default settings GLIMPSE2 v2.0.0's `--bam-file` path skips panel-monomorphic sites during its scan pass but not during parsing, and aborts every chunk with "AC/AN INFO fields in VCF are inconsistent with GT field" (54,406 such sites on chromosome 22). The documented workaround is `--keep-monomorphic-ref-sites`, which we did not test; we instead pre-filtered the panel, for GLIMPSE2 on chromosome 22 and for both tools on chromosomes 20, 10 and 1. Selphi 2 accepts the unfiltered panel, and GLIMPSE2's VCF-likelihood path is unaffected. Fifth, Selphi 2's likelihood-file reader treats records without a `PL` field as flat-likelihood sites, so a `PL` VCF carrying genotype-only indel records loses accuracy (chromosome 20, HG004: 92.49% against 98.07% non-reference concordance with those records removed); likelihood files should contain only records with likelihoods, as here, or the BAM path should be used. Sixth, heterozygote precision on chromosome 10 is lower than GLIMPSE2's in both Selphi 2 arms (native -0.48 pp, three of six samples), the one metric on one chromosome in Table 1d where Selphi 2 does not lead. Seventh, QUILT2 was not run on the capture libraries. Eighth, under default settings the low-coverage engine's conditioning set is largely independent of the target: the PBWT-selected union is capped in haplotype-index order and, because it covers nearly the whole panel, the cap keeps an almost fixed low-index subset, so the per-haplotype rare-carrier augmentation carries the target-specific information (Methods). A rank-order cap recovers the rarest bin at 0.5$\times$ but lowers other bins; a cap that keeps both the diversity of the fixed subset and the local specificity of the PBWT ranking remains to be designed and measured. Finally, a seventh capture library (NA12878) was excluded for the reason given in Methods.

**Privacy considerations.** Imputation output can leak information about the reference panel: each dosage is a deterministic function of the panel haplotypes the model copies, and adaptively chosen queries can reconstruct panel haplotypes from an imputation server's output^31^. Two features of Selphi 2 bear on this. Unlike Selphi 1's whole-chromosome PBWT pass, Selphi 2 matches within large overlapping windows (80 cM by default), which bounds the longest contiguous panel segment one query can expose, the quantity segment-linking attacks exploit. For sensitive panels, the panel can also be held under controlled access, as the TOPMed and MESA data used here are, and per-query output aggregated or rate-limited. We did not perform a formal privacy analysis; quantifying the reconstruction risk of windowed matching against a whole-chromosome scan is a question for server deployments.

The NYGC 1000 Genomes 30$\times$ GRCh38 call set (public) is the source of the 2,398-sample panel used for the capture-library benchmark; the brain-genomics-public 30$\times$ GIAB alignments (public) underlie Tables 2 and 2b; the gnomAD HGDP and 1000 Genomes harmonized callset^29^ is the source of the 6,332-haplotype panel; the GIAB v4.2.1 truth sets and the per-sample high-confidence BED files named in Methods are public. The six consumer-array and matched high-coverage whole-genome sequences used for the consumer-array benchmark (Results, Table 5) are de-identified genomes of consenting individuals and cannot be redistributed; the processing and filtering steps applied to them are given in full in the Methods so the analysis can be reproduced on equivalent array-plus-WGS data.

# **Code availability**

The Selphi 2 source code is openly available for academic and non-commercial use under a non-commercial license, consistent with the terms under which Selphi 1 was released, at https://github.com/omicsedge/selphi (branch `new_version/selphi2_cluster`). The repository provides the source, a self-contained binary, documentation, and the reference-panel preparation utilities required to reproduce the analyses in this work. The binary is fully self-contained and requires no container runtime, root privileges, or external tools, and is provided for x86-64 (with AVX2 and AVX-512 kernels) and ARM64 (NEON), including a statically linked build for older-glibc clusters.
